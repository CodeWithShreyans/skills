# Career context: use only when requested

These essays offer strategies and personal experiences, not representative labor
statistics or promises. Match suggestions to the person's time, location, access
needs, finances, and preferences. Verify present-day job listings, tool availability,
and employment arrangements rather than reusing historical specifics.

## S071 — Lessons Learned Speaking at Conferences

Source: https://www.joshwcomeau.com/career/lessons-learned-speaking-at-conferences/

**Use:** Choose a useful audience-specific idea, match it to the event, and iterate
the proposal and talk with feedback. Rehearse transitions and delivery as well as
the technical content; prepare a fallback for live demos.

**Watch:** Rejection is not a reliable measure of an idea's quality. Travel,
preparation time, and repeated commitments impose real costs. The article's later
reflection warns against overcommitting and supports reusing material.

**Check:** A clear audience takeaway, timed rehearsal, accessible slides, demo backup,
and a sustainable preparation schedule. Verify event terms before committing.

## S078 — Becoming a Software Developer Without a CS Degree

Source: https://www.joshwcomeau.com/career/no-cs-degree-required/

**Use:** Consider multiple entry paths: an internal role transition, an internship,
community relationships, relevant projects, freelancing, or demonstrable specialist
skills. Select a feasible strategy that builds on existing strengths.

**Watch:** The source explicitly describes a nonrepresentative collection of
stories. Its category percentages are not hiring probabilities. Side projects and
unpaid activity assume time/resources that not everyone has.

**Check:** Whether the proposed path fits the person's constraints and produces
credible evidence of ability without promising a job or prescribing uncompensated work.

## S083 — Finding your first remote job

Source: https://www.joshwcomeau.com/career/remote-work-pt2/

**Use:** Evaluate remote practice, not only the label on a vacancy: distribution
of staff and leadership, written decisions, meeting inclusion, time-zone overlap,
mentorship, and access to resources. Demonstrate relevant asynchronous collaboration.

**Watch:** A hybrid organization can make remote staff second-class participants.
Historical listings, compensation examples, and cross-border arrangements are not
current recommendations or legal guidance.

**Check:** Ask for concrete onboarding, feedback, promotion, and meeting examples.
Match optional portfolio/community work to available time and verify employment
details with appropriate current sources.

## S084 — My experience as a remote worker

Source: https://www.joshwcomeau.com/career/remote-work/

**Use:** Design an intentional work environment and communication rhythm. Remote
work can include an office or coworking space; separate location flexibility from
assumptions about schedules, isolation, or reduced collaboration.

**Watch:** The author's positive experiences reflect particular organizations and
personal preferences. Visibility, mentorship, social connection, and promotion
fairness need deliberate support rather than being assumed.

**Check:** How help is requested, how decisions become discoverable, how focus time
is protected, and how remote staff receive feedback and career opportunities.
